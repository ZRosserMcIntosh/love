# The mathematics in plain language

## The entire idea in one sentence

Love is not fuel. It can be the reason ordinary fuel—food, electricity, time,
muscle, and machines—gets pointed toward one person or one purpose.

Think of energy as water already inside pipes. Love does not manufacture the
water. It may turn a valve.

## Why there are two energy numbers

Imagine you spend an evening building something for someone.

Your body would have burned energy even if you had watched television. That
energy was **redirected**, but not necessarily newly consumed.

The computer may have remained off if you had watched television. Its
electricity may be genuinely **additional**.

That gives us:

1. **Allocated energy:** all measured energy used during the love-directed act,
   multiplied by how confidently we attribute the act to that motive.
2. **Induced energy:** only the difference between what happened and what would
   probably have happened instead, multiplied by the same attribution.

Neither number is “how much love.”

## The attribution dial

The causal-attribution coefficient \(a\) is a dial from zero to one:

- \(a=0\): the action would have happened for unrelated reasons.
- \(a=0.5\): available evidence assigns half of the causal credit to the
  targeted motive under the chosen model.
- \(a=1\): the action would not have happened without that motive.

It is an estimate that should carry uncertainty. It is not a mind-reading
machine.

## How every calculation function works

### kilocaloriesToJoules

**Question:** How much physical energy is represented by food Calories?

One dietary kilocalorie equals 4,184 joules:

\[
J=kcal\times4184
\]

Example:

\[
500\ kcal\times4184=2{,}092{,}000\ J
\]

### kilowattHoursToJoules

**Question:** How many joules did an electrical device use?

One kilowatt-hour equals 3,600,000 joules:

\[
J=kWh\times3{,}600{,}000
\]

Example:

\[
0.1\ kWh=360{,}000\ J
\]

### tonsTntToJoules and joulesToTonsTnt

**Question:** What familiar explosive-energy magnitude equals these joules?

\[
J=tons_{TNT}\times4{,}184{,}000{,}000
\]

Reverse the conversion by dividing. This compares total energy only. A
six-month project does not behave like an explosion and does not contain all
the expended energy. Most of its energy eventually disperses as heat.

### metabolicEnergyFromMets

**Question:** Approximately how much energy did a body use during an activity?

The standard population approximation treats one MET as one kilocalorie per
kilogram per hour:

\[
J=MET\times mass_{kg}\times hours\times4184
\]

For a 70 kg person doing 1.3-MET desk work for 2,160 hours:

\[
1.3\times70\times2160\times4184
=
822{,}407{,}040\ J
\]

This is an illustrative estimate. Real people have different resting
metabolism.

### incrementalMetabolicEnergyFromMets

**Question:** How much more body energy did the activity use than the stated
alternative?

\[
\Delta J
=(MET_{actual}-MET_{alternative})
\times mass_{kg}\times hours\times4184
\]

Using 1.3 MET instead of 1.0 MET:

\[
(1.3-1.0)\times70\times2160\times4184
\approx189{,}786{,}240\ J
\]

If the actual choice uses less energy than the alternative, the result is
negative. That is not an error; it records an energy saving.

### electricalEnergyFromPower

**Question:** How much electricity did a device use?

Power tells us energy per second. There are 3,600 seconds in an hour:

\[
J=watts\times hours\times3600
\]

A 50-watt computer used for 2,160 hours:

\[
50\times2160\times3600
=388{,}800{,}000\ J
\]

### attributedAllocatedEnergy

**Question:** How much actual energy do we assign to the targeted motive?

\[
E_{allocated}=aE_{actual}
\]

If the action used 1,000 J and attribution is 0.8:

\[
0.8\times1000=800\ J
\]

### attributedInducedEnergy

**Question:** How much additional energy do we assign to the targeted motive?

\[
E_{induced}=a(E_{actual}-E_{alternative})
\]

If actual use is 1,000 J, the alternative is 600 J, and attribution is 0.8:

\[
0.8(1000-600)=320\ J
\]

### averagePowerWatts

**Question:** How intensely was energy being expended on average?

\[
W=\frac{J}{seconds}
\]

Energy is the total amount. Power is the rate. A bomb and a long human project
can be compared in joules while being utterly different in power.

### summarizeEnergyLedger

**Question:** How do we add several sources without counting the same energy
twice?

The ledger adds independent root inputs. A brain-energy estimate marked as a
child of whole-body metabolism is displayed but not added again, because the
brain is already part of the body.

### equivalentProjectCount

**Question:** How many projects of this size equal a chosen reference energy?

\[
N=\frac{E_{reference}}{E_{project}}
\]

This is simple division, not a claim that the events have the same physical
effects or moral meaning.

### downstreamEnergyGain

**Question:** How much later physical energy did the initiating work cause or
redirect?

\[
G=\frac{E_{downstream}}{E_{initiating}}
\]

This measures energetic leverage. It still says nothing by itself about whether
the downstream result was wise, useful, consensual, or good.

## The six-month result

Under the repository's illustrative assumptions:

- Body energy during work: approximately 822 MJ
- Body energy above quiet rest: approximately 190 MJ
- Computer electricity: approximately 389 MJ
- Allocated direct project energy: approximately 1.21 GJ
- Induced direct project energy: approximately 579 MJ

The first number says how much energy was directed through the work boundary.
The second says how much extra energy was used compared with quiet rest and an
off computer. Change the alternative and the induced result changes.

That sensitivity is not a weakness to hide. It is the central scientific fact
the model must expose.
