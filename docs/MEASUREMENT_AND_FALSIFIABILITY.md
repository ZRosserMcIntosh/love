# Measurement and falsifiability

## Measurement hierarchy

Prefer methods in approximately this order, while matching the method to the
timescale:

1. Direct or indirect calorimetry for controlled short-duration body-energy
   measurement
2. Doubly labelled water for multi-day free-living total expenditure
3. Individually calibrated heart-rate and accelerometry models
4. Activity-specific population MET estimates for illustration
5. Uncalibrated consumer wearables only with explicit error bounds

For external energy:

1. Revenue-grade or calibrated electrical meters
2. Device-level watt meters
3. Vehicle fuel or battery telemetry
4. Utility and cloud-provider records
5. Engineering estimates with declared uncertainty

For embodied energy, use a documented life-cycle-assessment boundary and never
mix average and marginal estimates without explanation.

## Cognitive energy

Whole-brain metabolism is not the same thing as the additional energy caused by
thinking about a specific person or project.

If whole-body metabolism is already counted, brain energy is contained within
it. Adding both creates double counting.

Task-specific brain-energy estimates require specialized measurements and a
matched resting or alternative-cognition condition. Feeling mentally exhausted
does not establish a large metabolic increment.

## Causal-attribution evidence

Potential evidence includes:

- prospective intention records made before outcomes are known;
- removal or introduction of extrinsic rewards;
- recipient-randomized helping tasks;
- within-person comparisons across recipients and contexts;
- behavioral persistence as energetic cost rises;
- preregistered predictions;
- independent coding of motive evidence;
- sensitivity bounds for mixed or unidentified motives.

Retrospective self-report alone is insufficient for a strong causal claim.

## Candidate pilot experiments

### Pilot A — Cost-response curve

Participants choose whether to perform calibrated cycle-ergometer or grip work
to deliver a standardized benefit to a loved person, friend, stranger, or
themselves.

Estimate:

\[
\Pr(action\mid energetic\ cost,recipient,benefit)
\]

The relationship-specific shift in cost elasticity is the outcome. It is not
automatically labeled love.

### Pilot B — Naturalistic project ledger

Track one voluntarily chosen project with:

- wall-power metering;
- time-resolved activity classification;
- individual resting-metabolism calibration;
- preregistered counterfactual activities;
- daily motive reports;
- blinded review of causal-attribution evidence.

Report allocated and induced energy under several counterfactual models.

### Pilot C — Caregiving energy transfer

Measure food energy, heating, transportation, and caregiver metabolism directed
toward a dependent recipient. Keep benefit, burden, consent or authorization,
and health effects as separate outcomes.

## Falsifiable hypotheses

### H1 — Recipient-specific cost elasticity

Holding recipient benefit constant, attachment category predicts a measurable
change in willingness as energetic cost rises.

Falsified if recipient identity has no reproducible effect after preregistered
controls.

### H2 — Behavioral energy predicts persistence beyond self-report

Measured cost-response behavior adds out-of-sample predictive value beyond
self-reported attachment intensity.

Falsified if it adds no reliable predictive information.

### H3 — Attribution estimates have convergent validity

Prospective reports, revealed-preference tasks, and independent behavioral
coding should correlate enough to support a common latent construct.

Falsified or narrowed if the methods do not converge.

### H4 — Allocated and induced energy are empirically distinct

Projects with similar allocated energy can have substantially different induced
energy under measured counterfactuals.

This should be straightforward to demonstrate; failure would challenge the
utility of the proposed distinction.

## Failure conditions for the field

The research program should be rejected, renamed, or radically narrowed if:

- causal attribution cannot be measured with useful reliability;
- results merely restate time spent or calories burned;
- the framework predicts nothing beyond existing constructs;
- mixed motives make the target non-identifiable in every realistic design;
- researchers routinely interpret expenditure as proof of love;
- ethical variables cannot be kept independent of energetic magnitude; or
- claimed novelty disappears under systematic literature review.

## Prohibited inference

The following inference is invalid:

\[
\text{more joules}\Rightarrow\text{more or better love}
\]

A person can waste energy spectacularly. Another can act with extraordinary
wisdom and love using very little. Agapodynamics can measure energetic
consequence and cost-response behavior; it cannot appoint itself judge of the
heart.
