# Landing-page explanation

## Objective

The landing page must let an intelligent twelve-year-old understand the idea in
ten seconds while letting a physicist inspect every assumption without hunting
through marketing fog.

Use progressive disclosure:

1. One sentence
2. One analogy
3. Two equations
4. One worked example
5. Full assumptions and uncertainty

## Hero

### Headline

**Love does not create energy. It chooses where energy goes.**

### Subheadline

Agapodynamics is an open research program for measuring the ordinary physical
energy that love-motivated action redirects—through bodies, machines, care,
creation, and sacrifice—without pretending that joules can measure the whole of
love.

### Primary actions

- See the two equations
- Explore the six-month example

## Section 1 — The valve

Energy already exists in food, electricity, fuel, and machines. Love is not a
new fuel. It may be the reason that existing energy is directed toward building
something, crossing a country, caring for a child, or remaining beside someone
when leaving would be easier.

Visual concept: a single reservoir feeding several possible paths, with one
path selected. Avoid hearts floating through equations.

## Section 2 — The two numbers

### Energy allocated

How much measured energy was directed through the action?

\[
E_L^{allocated}=aE^{actual}
\]

### Energy induced

How much additional energy did the action cause compared with what would
otherwise have happened?

\[
E_L^{induced}=a(E^{actual}-E^{alternative})
\]

Explain \(a\) immediately:

> \(a\) is a cautious estimate of causal attribution—from zero, meaning the
> action would have happened anyway, to one, meaning it would not have happened
> without the targeted motive.

## Section 3 — Why the distinction matters

If you spend an evening coding for someone, your body would still have burned
energy while watching television. The body's energy may have been redirected
more than increased. But a computer that would otherwise have been off creates
additional electrical expenditure.

The framework reports both. It does not quietly choose whichever number sounds
more dramatic.

## Section 4 — Worked project

Display assumptions before the result:

- 70 kg person
- 12 work hours per day
- 180 days
- 1.3-MET desk-work approximation
- 1.0-MET quiet-rest alternative
- 50-watt computer
- attribution coefficient initially set to 1.0

Then show:

- 822 MJ of body energy allocated during work
- 190 MJ of body energy above quiet rest
- 389 MJ of computer electricity
- 1.21 GJ allocated within the direct project boundary
- 579 MJ induced above the stated alternative

Every number should expand to reveal its arithmetic.

## Section 5 — The atomic-scale thought experiment

One ton of TNT is conventionally equivalent to 4.184 GJ. The illustrative
project's allocated energy is therefore approximately 0.289 tons of TNT in raw
energy magnitude.

Then state, prominently:

> Equal joules do not mean equal power, behavior, storage, or consequence. A
> weapon releases enormous energy rapidly to destroy order. Human work expends
> modest power over months while potentially building order, information, care,
> and protection.

Never use an explosion animation. The point is the contrast between destructive
release and constructive direction.

## Section 6 — What this does not claim

- Love is not a newly discovered force.
- More expenditure does not prove more love.
- Waste, coercion, obsession, and fear can consume energy too.
- Quiet love may expend very little additional energy.
- Benefit, harm, consent, risk, and meaning remain separate measurements.

This section is not legal boilerplate. It is part of the theory.

## Section 7 — How it could become science

Show the measurement chain:

1. Measure actual physical energy
2. State the comparison world
3. Estimate causal attribution
4. prevent double counting
5. propagate uncertainty
6. measure recipient benefit and harm separately
7. preregister and attempt falsification

## Section 8 — Open ledger

Publish a claim table with status labels:

- Established
- Adapted
- Proposed
- Speculative
- Falsified

Visitors should be able to challenge individual claims rather than accepting a
single grand narrative.

## Voice

The writing should feel intellectually fearless, mathematically sober, and
human. It may be poetic at the entrance, but every poetic statement must hand
the reader cleanly to a measurable claim or an explicit nonclaim.
