# Foundations

## Working definition

**Agapodynamics** is the proposed quantitative study of physical energy that is
causally allocated, additionally induced, transferred, conserved, or redirected
by voluntary action intended to advance the flourishing of another person.

The construct is intentionally narrower than love itself. It measures physical
consequences of love-motivated action. It does not measure the full reality,
depth, truth, or moral worth of love.

## Founding proposition

**Love does not create energy. Love selects its destination.**

Food, stored chemical energy, electricity, fuel, and existing infrastructure
supply the energy. Love may be one cause determining which path that energy
takes through a person and through the surrounding world.

## Axioms

### A0 — Conservation

Agapodynamics introduces no new physical energy and does not modify conservation
of energy.

### A1 — Direction rather than creation

The object of study is the causal direction of ordinary physical energy into
actions, not an invisible substance called love-energy.

### A2 — Counterfactual dependence

No induced-energy claim is meaningful without a stated alternative:

> What would this person and the surrounding system have done over the same
> interval if this attachment or commitment had not directed the action?

### A3 — Declared boundary

Every result must identify what it includes:

- person-bound metabolism;
- direct operational electricity or fuel;
- embodied production energy;
- energy delivered to a recipient; or
- downstream energy later caused or saved.

### A4 — No double counting

Energy may change form many times without becoming new energy. Chemical energy
used by the brain becomes electrochemical activity and heat; counting all three
as independent inputs would be false.

### A5 — Attribution is uncertain

The causal-attribution coefficient is inferred from evidence. It is not directly
observed and is not a love-intensity score.

### A6 — Moral value is not a joule

Consent, wisdom, benefit, harm, grief, opportunity cost, loyalty, risk, and
meaning require separate variables. Physics does not assign moral signs to
joules.

### A7 — Expenditure is not proof

Obsession, coercion, guilt, fear, status seeking, inefficiency, or pathology can
produce high expenditure. Low-energy actions can embody extraordinary love.

### A8 — Uncertainty travels with the result

Measurements, counterfactuals, attribution, and system boundaries all create
uncertainty. A serious result is a distribution or interval, not decorative
precision.

## Core quantities

For flow \(i\):

- \(E_i^A\): actual energy in joules;
- \(E_i^C\): counterfactual energy in joules;
- \(a_i\): causal-attribution coefficient in \([0,1]\);
- \(T_i\): duration in seconds.

### Allocated energy

\[
E_{L,i}^{allocated}=a_iE_i^A
\]

This measures how much actual physical-energy throughput is attributed to the
love-directed act.

### Induced energy

\[
E_{L,i}^{induced}=a_i(E_i^A-E_i^C)
\]

This measures additional energy relative to the declared alternative. It may be
negative if love motivates a lower-energy action.

### Average attributed power

\[
\bar P_{L,i}=\frac{E_{L,i}}{T_i}
\]

Power distinguishes an intense short action from a quiet commitment sustained
for years.

### Downstream energetic gain

\[
G_E=\frac{E_{downstream}}{E_{initiating}}
\]

This is a causal energy-leverage ratio, not a benefit score. A software system
may redirect substantial later electricity or transportation while its human
benefit remains a different question.

## Minimum result vector

A credible study should not collapse everything into one number. The minimum
result should resemble:

\[
\mathcal A =
(E_{allocated},E_{induced},T,a,B,H,C,U)
\]

where:

- \(B\) is recipient benefit measured in an appropriate domain;
- \(H\) is harm;
- \(C\) is consent or authorization status; and
- \(U\) is uncertainty.

Only the first two coordinates necessarily use joules.

## Necessary distinctions

### Love versus motive report

A participant reporting “I did it for love” is evidence, not proof. Motivation
may be mixed, retrospective, strategic, or partially unconscious.

### Love versus attachment

Attachment can motivate protection, dependency, control, sacrifice, or harm.
Agapodynamics must define which construct is being studied rather than treating
all attachment-driven expenditure as healthy love.

### Recipient benefit versus actor intention

Good intention and good outcome are independent. Both belong in the model.

### Sacrifice versus inefficiency

Expending more energy than necessary is not automatically more sacrificial.
Sacrifice concerns the actor's lost alternatives, burdens, and risks. Those
variables must be recorded directly instead of smuggled into joules.

## Scientific status

Agapodynamics is presently a proposed integrative framework. Its novelty,
construct validity, measurement reliability, and predictive usefulness are all
open questions.
