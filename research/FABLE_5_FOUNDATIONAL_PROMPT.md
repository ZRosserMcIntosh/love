# I. Inviolable starting constraints

These are scope constraints, not conclusions you must defend:

1. Do not describe love as a new physical energy, field, force, particle,
   frequency, vibration, or thermodynamic substance.
2. Ordinary physical energy remains ordinary physical energy and obeys standard
   conservation laws.
3. The proposed research object is the causal allocation, induction, transfer,
   saving, and redirection of measurable physical energy through behavior and
   technical systems.
4. Joules cannot directly measure moral goodness, emotional depth, consent,
   loyalty, grief, dignity, wisdom, or recipient flourishing.
5. High expenditure is not proof of love. Obsession, coercion, fear, guilt,
   status seeking, waste, or pathology can cause high expenditure.
6. Low-energy action may embody extraordinary love.
7. Every numerical conclusion must state its system boundary, counterfactual,
   causal assumptions, units, uncertainty, and double-counting policy.
8. If the entire program is merely existing bioenergetics plus motive labels,
   say so precisely and recommend abandoning or renaming it.

The sentence motivating the inquiry is:

> Love does not create energy. Love selects its destination.

Treat that sentence as a hypothesis-generating intuition, not a scientific law.

---

# II. Research question

Determine whether it is scientifically coherent and empirically useful to ask:

> How much measurable physical energy was allocated, additionally induced,
> transferred, conserved, or redirected in a particular direction—and would
> not have taken that direction, under a declared causal model, but for a
> specified love-related commitment?

Do not silently convert this into the easier question “How many calories did an
activity burn?” The central difficulty is causal redirection under mixed motives
and unobservable counterfactuals.

# VI. Core mathematical problem

Begin with continuous time.

Let:

- \(P_i^A(t)\) be actual physical power of flow \(i\);
- \(P_i^C(t)\) be counterfactual physical power;
- \(a_i(t)\in[0,1]\) be a causal-attribution estimate;
- \(B_i(t)\) be recipient benefit in a domain-specific unit;
- \(H_i(t)\) be harm;
- \(C_i(t)\) encode consent or legitimate authorization;
- \(\mathcal B\) be the declared system boundary.

The repository proposes:

\[
E_{L,i}^{allocated}
=
\int a_i(t)P_i^A(t)\,dt
\]

\[
E_{L,i}^{induced}
=
\int a_i(t)\left(P_i^A(t)-P_i^C(t)\right)\,dt
\]

Interrogate these equations rather than accepting them.

For each equation:

1. Derive it from explicit definitions.
2. Perform dimensional analysis.
3. State identifiability conditions.
4. State boundary assumptions.
5. State when the result can be negative.
6. Show how double counting occurs.
7. Identify whether multiplying by \(a\) is mathematically and causally
   defensible.
8. Propose superior alternatives.
9. Give at least one counterexample where the equation misleads.
10. Provide uncertainty propagation.

Investigate whether attribution should instead be represented by:

- potential-outcome contrasts;
- stochastic interventions;
- mediation analysis;
- attributable fractions;
- structural causal models;
- causal Shapley values;
- principal stratification;
- partial identification and bounds;
- Bayesian model averaging;
- information-theoretic responsibility measures.

Do not force a point estimate if only bounds are identifiable.

# VII. Mixed-motive decomposition

Let the motive set be:

\[
\mathcal M=
\{love,duty,money,status,fear,guilt,habit,reciprocity,\ldots\}
\]

Solve or bound the problem of allocating causal responsibility when motives:

- interact synergistically;
- substitute for one another;
- are each sufficient;
- are each necessary only in coalition;
- change over time;
- are reported dishonestly;
- are partly unconscious;
- are themselves caused by the relationship.

Investigate Shapley decomposition:

\[
\phi_m
=
\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal M\setminus\{m\}}
\frac{|S|!(|\mathcal M|-|S|-1)!}{|\mathcal M|!}
\left[v(S\cup\{m\})-v(S)\right]
\]

Ask:

1. What should coalition value \(v(S)\) represent—probability of action,
   expected energy, recipient benefit, or something else?
2. Are Shapley axioms normatively or psychologically appropriate?
3. Can the necessary counterfactual motive coalitions ever be observed?
4. Would cooperative-game attribution create false precision?
5. Can monotonicity be assumed?
6. What bounds follow without monotonicity?
7. What experiments could identify interaction terms?

Provide at least two non-Shapley alternatives and compare their properties.

# X. Counterfactual architecture

For a person working on a six-month project, compare at least these
counterfactuals:

- quiet rest;
- television or ordinary leisure;
- different paid work;
- an unrelated personal project;
- the same project for money;
- the same project for another recipient;
- no project but unchanged computer use;
- a lower-energy implementation of the same benefit.

For each, calculate how induced energy changes while allocated energy remains
similar or changes differently.

Develop a “counterfactual sensitivity surface”:

\[
E_L^{induced}(a,E_C)
=
a(E_A-E_C)
\]

Show partial derivatives:

\[
\frac{\partial E_L}{\partial a}=E_A-E_C
\qquad
\frac{\partial E_L}{\partial E_C}=-a
\]

Explain what these mean scientifically and in language a child can understand.

Determine whether multiple counterfactuals should be reported as:

- scenarios;
- model averaging;
- an identified set;
- a robustness curve;
- or a posterior distribution.

# XX. Quality controls

Before finalizing:

1. Run a dimensional audit of every equation.
2. Recalculate every numerical example independently.
3. Trace every citation to a real source.
4. Mark every claim as established, adapted, proposed, speculative, falsified,
   or unresolved.
5. List unresolved contradictions.
6. Distinguish empirical results from analogies.
7. Remove poetic language that masquerades as physics.
8. Restore plain-language explanations wherever rigor became unreadable.
9. State your confidence in each major conclusion.

The desired outcome is not approval. The desired outcome is a framework that
has survived the strongest attack you can mount—or a clear, useful explanation
of why it should die.
